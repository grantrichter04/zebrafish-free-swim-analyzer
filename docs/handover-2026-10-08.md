# Handover, 8 October 2026

Where the "make this easy for RAs" work stands, for whoever picks it up next.
Everything below is on branch `polish/ra-ready`, which is local only: nothing
has been pushed, at the owner's request, so CI has not run on any of it.

## The goal

A research assistant with no command-line experience opens one app on a shared
lab laptop and takes free-swim videos from recording to results. The question
the results should answer: is one group of fish more active than another?

The owner's standing preferences:

- Elegant over exhaustive. Remove what is not useful instead of hiding it.
- Build in small slices he can try from the desktop shortcut, then react.
- The tank (session) is the experimental unit, not the fish.

## How to run it

- Conda env `freeswim` (Python 3.12, torch cu128, idtrackerai 6.0.14) holds
  both idtracker.ai and the analyzer. `install.bat` builds it.
- Desktop shortcut "Free Swim Analyzer" runs `launch.pyw` from this repo.
- Tests: `%USERPROFILE%\.conda\envs\freeswim\python.exe -m pytest -q`
  (267 passing at the time of writing).
- The owner's older `idtrackerai` conda env is his own; leave it alone.

## Decisions already made (do not reopen without a reason)

- One conda env, one shared Windows login, installed once by `install.bat`.
- Setups (`.toml`) live beside the videos; one setup per folder of videos.
- Units: centimetres preferred; otherwise one body length for the whole
  experiment. Per-video body length was dropped because idtracker.ai's value
  moves with lighting (73.2 and 79.7 px for two videos from one rig).
- Freezing is off the results screens until someone validates the threshold
  against video. The idea: mark immobile fish in the Video Inspector with an
  adjustable threshold.
- The validator is a spot check, not a to-do list. "Miss id" gaps and most
  "Jump" rows need no action; measured on real data they change nothing.
- No 95th percentile; the cumulative speed curve shows every percentile.

## The segmentation investigation (in progress)

The owner filmed three lighting set-ups in a smaller container (videos under
`...\Free Swimming\810 open field test videos smaller container\`) and could
not get idtracker.ai to segment the fish.

What was found, all measured on those videos:

- idtracker.ai's mask with background subtraction is
  `absdiff(background, frame) > threshold`. His two attempts were at 202 (no
  blobs: fish differ from the background by at most about 90) and 18 (fish
  plus their shadows and reflections). The usable window is roughly 25-45.
- He had not set a Region of interest or the number of animals.
- "Light pointed to left corner 2" segments far better than the other two.
  "Lamp pointed downward" is the worst.
- An alternative mask (only pixels darker than the background, difference
  taken relative to the background brightness, slight blur) raised the share
  of frames with exactly six separate blobs from 59% to 71%, 37-41% to
  42-44%, and 11% to 21% on the three videos, and works over a wider
  threshold range. A real but moderate gain.
- It is not known whether there are six or seven fish. This was asked and not
  yet answered, and the scores above assume six.

`scripts/score_segmentation.py <folder> <n_fish>` reproduces the comparison and
also scores morphological clean-up and a background-free black-hat method.
Share of 150 sampled frames with exactly six blobs, at each method's best
threshold:

| Method | lamp downward | left corner 2 | zebrabox |
|---|---|---|---|
| idtracker.ai: abs(frame - background) | 14.7% | 60.0% | 31.3% |
| relative to background, blurred | 20.0% | 71.3% | 42.0% |
| + morphological open | 19.3% | 70.0% | 40.0% |
| + morphological close | 20.7% | 71.3% | 42.7% |
| black-hat, no background at all | 42.7% | 28.0% | 22.7% |
| relative x black-hat | 20.0% | 73.3% | 44.7% |

Reading it: the relative mask is the consistent gain; morphological clean-up
adds nothing; combining with black-hat adds a point or two. Black-hat alone is
the only thing that helps the worst-lit video (42.7% against 14.7%), which
suggests the median background is what fails there, but it is poor on the
other two. No method tested gets a badly lit video near the 71% the best
lighting reaches. (These idtracker.ai figures differ slightly from the ones
above because this run stepped the threshold by 4, not 5.)

What was proposed and awaits a yes:

- An opt-in "mask for uneven lighting" when making a setup. Feasible because
  `idtrackerai.base.animals_detection.segmentation.process_frame` is the one
  function both the setup window and tracking use. Two catches: tracking runs
  segmentation in spawned worker processes, so the replacement must be applied
  at import time of a launcher script (tracking currently runs
  `python -m idtrackerai.start` directly, see `tracking.build_track_command`);
  and the choice has to be stored beside the setup, because idtracker.ai
  rejects unknown keys in a `.toml`.
- A "setup check" on the Tracking tab: "N of 150 sampled frames show 6
  separate fish", before a long batch. It would also rank lighting set-ups.

## Things that will bite

- **idtracker.ai is patched from outside** in
  `fish_analyzer/idtrackerai_setup_window.py` (its window class is not a
  public interface). `install.bat` pins idtrackerai to 6.0.x for that reason.
- **idtracker.ai exits with code 0 even when tracking fails.** Success is
  judged by `trajectories.npy` appearing.
- **Real data is in a OneDrive/SharePoint-synced tree**, and the owner renames
  folders there. The first experiment is now
  `...\Free Swimming\<first experiment>\`.
  Local copies of its two videos are in `C:\projects\freeswim-test-data\`.
- **Fish numbering differs between old and new tabs.** The old tabs count from
  0; Results and Shoaling use idtracker.ai's labels, which start at 1.
- **A drawn tank outline is not saved**: it lasts until the app closes.
- **An arena is stored in the session's unit** as well as in pixels.
  `_set_calibration` in `gui/data_tab.py` rescales it when the unit changes.
- **In this Git Bash tool, backslashes inside a heredoc are halved.** Scripts
  containing `\n`, `\\` or regexes written that way come out wrong. Write
  them to a file with the editor instead.

## Findings on the first real data (one tank per group, so observations only)

- Treated-tank fish 6 sits nearly still for about four and a half minutes. The
  owner confirmed in the validator that this is a real fish.
- Time near the wall: control 57.6%, treated 3.9%, against 46% for even use
  of the tank. Checked independently in raw pixels and by drawing the outline
  and positions on a frame of each video; the outline matches the tank in both.
- Both tanks shoal at about half the random-placement distance.
- A 10-minute, 1288x964 video takes about 9 minutes to track on the RTX 4070
  Ti SUPER.
- Re-encoding the camera's MJPEG losslessly makes files 3.4 to 4.2 times
  larger, so it was dropped.
